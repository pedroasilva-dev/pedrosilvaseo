How to Appear in LLMs? How to Optimize Your Website for AI

Discover how to increase your website's visibility across ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI and other artificial intelligence systems through technical SEO, content, authority, entities and AI Search optimization.

Pedro Silva · August 15, 2026 · 9 min read

What does it mean to appear in LLMs?

Online search is changing. For years, the main concern of an SEO strategy was getting a page to rank among the top Google search results.

With the evolution of tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI-powered search experiences, there is now a new question: how can a brand or website be discovered, understood and mentioned by AI systems?

It is important to understand that appearing in LLMs does not necessarily mean that a page has to appear as a traditional search result.

An AI system can use information from different sources to build an answer and, depending on the experience, present links, citations or references to the websites it used.

The goal of AI optimization is not to find a “trick” to get into ChatGPT. It is to build a website that is technically accessible, semantically clear, relevant, trustworthy and authoritative enough to be considered a useful source of information.

How do LLMs and AI Search work?

Before discussing optimization, it is important to distinguish between two concepts that are often confused: LLMs and AI Search.

A Large Language Model, or LLM, is a model trained to understand and generate language. ChatGPT and Gemini are examples of products that use models of this type.

An AI Search experience can combine a generative model with search engines, information retrieval systems and external sources to answer questions that require current or specific information.

This means that optimizing a website for AI is not simply about “optimizing for an LLM”.

In practice, we are trying to improve the ability of the search and AI ecosystem to:

  • Discover the website.
  • Access important pages.
  • Understand the content.
  • Identify entities and relationships.
  • Evaluate relevance and authority.
  • Use the information when it is relevant to a question.

Is SEO still important for appearing in LLMs?

Yes. One of the biggest mistakes in discussions about AI Search is assuming that SEO is no longer relevant.

Search systems still need to discover, access and understand pages. As a result, basic problems with crawling, indexing or architecture can limit the visibility of a website regardless of the quality of its content.

SEO is not replaced by GEO or AI Search. Technical fundamentals continue to form the foundation.

SEO is still the foundation

  • Information architecture
  • Useful content
  • Internal linking
  • Authority and trust
  • Structured data
  • Performance and user experience

How do I optimize my website for AI?

There is no single implementation that can turn a website into an “AI-optimized website”.

Optimization should be seen as a combination of technical, editorial and authority improvements that make information easier to discover, interpret and contextualize.

There are, however, several fundamental pillars that should form part of any strategy.

1. Make sure your website can be crawled

The first step is to make sure that search engines can find and access your important pages.

Check your robots.txt, meta robots directives, HTTP status codes, internal links, sitemaps and any potential crawling restrictions.

2. Make sure your important pages can be indexed

A page can be crawlable without being available in a search engine's index.

It is important to analyze canonicals, noindex directives, duplicate versions, URL parameters and indexing issues.

3. Create content that directly answers questions

Content written simply to reach a certain word count is not a strong strategy.

A page should clearly address the user's intent, explain the subject in sufficient depth and avoid unnecessary information.

Clear subheadings, lists, examples, definitions and direct answers can also help organize information.

4. Demonstrate real-world experience and knowledge

Generic content exists in huge quantities across the Web. One way to differentiate a website is by demonstrating real experience.

Original examples, data, case studies, detailed explanations and well-supported opinions can make content more useful and distinctive.

5. Build topical authority

Instead of publishing isolated pages about dozens of unrelated topics, it is preferable to develop depth within areas that are relevant to the business.

Create related content and connect it through a logical internal linking architecture.

6. Use structured data correctly

Schema Markup allows certain elements of a page to be described using structured vocabularies.

Depending on the content, types such as Article, Person, Organization, Product, BreadcrumbList and other appropriate types can be used.

However, structured data does not guarantee that a website will be cited by an AI system. It should represent accurate information that is visible on the page.

7. Make entities and relationships clear

A page should not be analyzed simply as a collection of keywords.

Modern systems need to understand people, organizations, products, concepts, places and relationships between different entities.

Therefore, it is important to clearly explain who the entity is, what it does, in which context it exists and how it relates to the rest of the website's content.

8. Strengthen authority beyond your website

Brand authority is not built exclusively through the pages on your own website.

Mentions, links, references, publications, professional profiles and other external sources can contribute to building a stronger digital presence.

How to create content optimized for AI Search

A content strategy for AI Search should start with search intent rather than trying to include as many keywords as possible.

A good page should be able to answer one main question while also providing enough context to address related questions.

Start with the answer

If someone searches for “what is technical SEO?”, the page should quickly explain the concept before going into detail.

It can then develop the different components, provide examples and answer related questions.

Use a logical structure

Headings should represent the actual structure of the content. H2 should be used for main topics and H3 for related subtopics.

Avoid superficial information

Creating ten articles that are almost identical around small keyword variations can result in redundant content.

It is preferable to have fewer pages, with each one clearly addressing a specific intent and covering the topic in depth.

Architecture and internal linking for AI

Website architecture helps establish relationships between pieces of content.

A website specializing in a particular area should be able to demonstrate that specialization through the organization of its own content.

For example, a website about SEO may have a main page about Technical SEO connected to pages covering crawling, indexing, JavaScript SEO, canonicals, sitemaps and Core Web Vitals.

These pieces of content form a topic cluster.

Internal linking helps communicate relationships between pages and allows users and search engines to navigate the content more easily.

Do not create internal links simply to add links. Link pages when there is a genuine contextual relationship between the pieces of content.

Authority, entities and trust

To understand why a source may be considered relevant, it is necessary to look beyond the individual page.

Who published the content? What is their experience? Does the organization exist? Is it mentioned by other sources? Is there consistent information about the entity?

These signals help build a more complete context around a brand or organization.

Therefore, a professional website should clearly present information about the company or person responsible for the content, areas of expertise, contact information and other relevant details.

What about Google AI Mode?

Google is also transforming the search experience through AI-powered features.

Google AI Mode enables more complex search interactions and answers that can combine information from different sources.

This reinforces the importance of the same fundamentals: useful content, strong architecture, crawlability, indexing, context and authority.

What not to do if you want to appear in LLMs

The search for new ways to appear in AI systems has also created many ideas about possible “tricks” to manipulate these systems.

A sustainable strategy should not depend on artificial techniques or repeatedly inserting specific words into a page.

  • Do not fill your content with keywords without context.
  • Do not create dozens of nearly identical pages.
  • Do not use Schema Markup to declare information that does not exist on the page.
  • Do not accidentally block crawling of important pages.
  • Do not publish automatically generated content without review and added value.
  • Do not assume that there is a technique that guarantees citations in a specific LLM.

How to measure visibility in LLMs

One of the biggest differences between traditional SEO and AI Search is measurement.

Rankings, impressions, clicks and CTR remain important metrics for Google, but they do not fully describe a brand's presence across generative search experiences.

An AI Search strategy can track indicators such as:

  • How often the brand is mentioned.
  • How often the website appears as a source.
  • Queries where the brand is recommended.
  • Competitors presented in answers.
  • Pages used as sources.
  • The context in which the brand is presented.
  • Changes in visibility over time.

It is important to track these signals consistently because a single search does not necessarily represent a brand's overall visibility.

Checklist: how to optimize a website for AI

If you are starting to work on your website's visibility across AI Search environments, use this checklist as a starting point.

AI Search checklist

  • Can the website be crawled?
  • Can important pages be indexed?
  • Is robots.txt correctly configured?
  • Are XML sitemaps up to date?
  • Are canonicals correct?
  • Is the website architecture logical?
  • Is there an internal linking strategy?
  • Does the content directly answer users' questions?
  • Do pages demonstrate experience and knowledge?
  • Are entities clearly identified?
  • Does structured data accurately represent the information?
  • Does the brand have authority and external references?
  • Are you monitoring the brand's presence in AI Search?

Conclusion: the future of organic visibility

The question “how to appear in LLMs?” will become increasingly relevant as people use artificial intelligence to discover information, compare options and make decisions.

However, the answer is not to abandon traditional SEO and look for a new set of “tricks”.

The fundamentals remain the foundation.

A website needs to be crawlable, indexable, technically sound, semantically clear and supported by useful content. At the same time, it needs to build authority and clearly demonstrate who is behind the published information.

The main change is that we should no longer think only about rankings.

The question becomes: how is my brand discovered, understood, selected and represented during a search?

This paradigm shift is at the heart of AI Search.

Frequently asked questions

How can I appear in LLMs?

There is no technique that guarantees visibility in an LLM. The best starting point is to ensure crawlability, indexing, useful content, authority, semantic context, strong architecture and structured information.

How do I optimize my website for AI?

Start with technical SEO fundamentals and then work on content, entities, authority, internal linking and information structure. Optimization should make the website easy to discover, understand and contextualize.

Can I guarantee that my website will appear in ChatGPT?

No. Visibility or citation depends on the query, the system, available sources and several other factors. No technique guarantees a specific position or citation.

Is SEO still important for AI Search?

Yes. Crawling, indexing, architecture, content, authority, internal links and other SEO fundamentals remain important for the discovery and understanding of websites.
