GEO: What Is Generative Engine Optimization?

Learn what GEO is, how it relates to SEO, and the key practices for increasing content visibility across AI Search and generative answers.

Pedro Silva · August 17, 2026 · 11 min read

Search is moving beyond a simple list of pages ranked by position. AI Search tools and experiences can combine information from multiple sources and present synthesized answers, with links to content supporting those answers.

This is where the concept of GEO — Generative Engine Optimization comes in. The term was introduced to describe practices designed to increase content visibility in answers generated by generative search engines. Initial academic research into GEO emerged in 2023, and the topic has evolved rapidly since then.

In short: GEO is not a magic replacement for SEO. It is a way of thinking about visibility in environments where AI systems retrieve, select, synthesize and cite information from the Web.

What is GEO?

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization. The concept focuses on improving the likelihood that a brand or page will be found, used or cited in answers produced by generative search systems.

Unlike a traditional SERP, where users see an ordered list of results, a generative answer can combine information from multiple sources before presenting an answer.

This changes the question from “where does my page rank?” to questions such as “is my brand being found?”, “is my content selected as a source?” and “is the information from my page being used in the answer?”

Is GEO the same as SEO?

No. GEO and SEO are related, but they are not synonyms.

SEO continues to focus on discovery, crawlability, indexing, relevance, quality, website architecture and authority across search engines. GEO adds a layer focused on how generative systems retrieve and present information.

In practice, there is significant overlap. A website that cannot be crawled, indexed or understood is unlikely to have a strong foundation for AI Search. This is why Technical SEO remains an important part of any generative visibility strategy.

How do generative search engines work?

Systems vary, but a generative search experience can involve several stages: interpreting the query, retrieving information, selecting sources, combining context and generating an answer.

In Google's search experiences, official documentation explains that AI Overviews and AI Mode remain connected to Google's core Search systems and can use multiple related searches to explore different aspects of a question.

For SEO professionals, this means visibility is no longer based solely on an exact match between a keyword and a page. Context, topical coverage, clarity and the ability to provide useful information become even more important.

What makes content good for GEO?

There is no universal set of “GEO tricks” that guarantees citations across every platform. Recent research shows that results can vary between engines, queries and contexts.

The strongest approach is to combine SEO fundamentals with content that is clear, factual, well structured and easy to interpret.

Clear answers

Start each section with a direct answer to the problem the reader is trying to solve. Definitions, steps, comparisons and objective explanations are easier to interpret than overly vague content.

Topical depth

Cover the subject comprehensively and connect related concepts. A single superficial article is unlikely to demonstrate the same depth as a coherent set of interconnected pages.

Verifiable information

Use data, examples, references and facts that can be verified. Trust is particularly important when an AI-generated answer needs to select sources.

Semantic structure

Use clear headings, well-defined paragraphs, lists, tables where appropriate, and HTML that correctly represents the structure of the information.

Entities, context and authority

A brand should not rely solely on a keyword to explain who it is and what its area of expertise is.

A GEO strategy can benefit from a coherent ecosystem of information about the company, experts, products, services, topics and relationships between entities.

This is where the concept of topical authority becomes useful. Instead of publishing many disconnected articles, create content covering different aspects of the same topic and connect those pages naturally.

LLMs need to find relevant information, understand the context of a page and relate it to search intent. Visibility therefore depends on a combination of content quality, technical accessibility, semantic context and authority.

These principles are explored in greater detail in the guide on how to appear in LLMs.

Internal linking and GEO

Internal linking is not only a technique for distributing internal authority. It also helps build context.

When a GEO page links to detailed content about Technical SEO, AI Mode and LLMs, it creates a semantic network that helps users navigate the website and helps crawlers discover related pages.

Internal links also help search engines discover other relevant pages and provide context about the relationship between different pieces of content.

Why does Technical SEO still matter?

GEO does not eliminate technical infrastructure. If an important page cannot be crawled, is blocked, is not indexed or cannot be properly interpreted, its ability to contribute to organic visibility is limited.

Google itself states that, for a page to be eligible as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, it must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search. There are no additional technical requirements specific to these features.

Therefore, before looking for specific GEO techniques, it is worth ensuring the fundamentals are in place: Technical SEO, useful content, architecture, internal linking and indexing.

Original content and experience

A GEO strategy should not become a factory for generic pages created solely to try to appear in AI-generated answers.

Google recommends helpful, reliable content created for people and warns against mass-producing pages without added value.

For a professional website, this means adding something other results do not have: first-hand experience, examples, data, informed opinions, processes, case studies or genuinely useful explanations.

Does Schema Markup help with GEO?

Structured data can help search engines understand certain elements of a page, provided it represents information that is accurate and visible on the page.

However, there is no “GEO Schema” that guarantees visibility in AI-generated answers. Google does not require a special structured data type to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.

The value of Schema is to complement the clarity of the information, not to replace quality, relevance or authority.

How to optimize a website for GEO

1. Start with SEO fundamentals

Fix crawling, indexing, canonicalization, architecture, performance and internal linking issues.

2. Create pages that answer real questions

Structure content around user intent and answer questions directly before going deeper into the topic.

3. Build topical clusters

Create a main page around a topic and supporting content that explores relevant subtopics in greater depth.

4. Make information easy to extract

Use clear definitions, steps, lists, comparisons, data and examples. Avoid hiding important information inside elements that are difficult to interpret.

5. Demonstrate experience and authority

Explain who is behind the content, use sources when necessary and add original knowledge and experience.

6. Build presence beyond your own website

In many AI Search contexts, information available outside a brand's own domain can also influence the context in which that brand is understood. Recent research suggests meaningful differences between engines and a significant role for third-party sources in certain environments, meaning a GEO strategy should also consider external authority.

How do you measure GEO?

Measuring GEO is more difficult than tracking a Google ranking because a generative answer can vary depending on the query, language, timing and context.

A practical approach is to create a fixed set of questions related to the business and monitor, at different points in time, whether the brand appears, which sources are cited, in what context it appears and which competitors are mentioned.

It is also important to separate different metrics: discovery, citation, influence on the answer and business outcomes. Recent GEO research recommends this separation instead of treating every form of visibility as the same.

On Google, this measurement is evolving. In June 2026, Google announced new performance reporting for generative features in Search Console, rolling out to a subset of websites, providing visibility into experiences such as AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Common GEO mistakes

  • Treating GEO as a replacement for SEO.
  • Creating hundreds of generic AI-generated pages without original value.
  • Artificially repeating keywords.
  • Believing that a single prompt or technique guarantees citations.
  • Ignoring Technical SEO, indexing and internal linking.
  • Measuring only mentions instead of business outcomes.

Is GEO the future of SEO?

It is more useful to think of GEO as an extension of search strategy rather than a discipline that replaces SEO.

Google AI Mode and other generative experiences are changing how users discover information. Google states that fundamental SEO practices remain relevant and that its AI experiences aim to provide links that allow users to explore content from different sources.

For brands, this means visibility now exists across multiple surfaces: traditional search results, AI Overviews, AI Mode and other generative platforms.

Conclusion

GEO is the optimization of visibility in an environment where answers can be built from multiple sources. But there is no “optimize for AI” button.

The foundations remain strong: Technical SEO, useful content, semantic context, authority, architecture and a high-quality user experience. The difference is that we now need to measure not only rankings and clicks, but also discovery, citations and presence in generative answers.

This is why SEO and GEO should be treated as complementary disciplines. A strong strategy prepares a website to be found, understood, selected and visited across different search environments.

Frequently asked questions

What is GEO?

GEO, or Generative Engine Optimization, is the optimization of content visibility in answers produced by generative search systems and AI Search.

Does GEO replace SEO?

No. GEO and SEO are complementary. Technical SEO, content, relevance, architecture and authority remain important.

How do you do GEO?

Start with SEO fundamentals, create useful and original content, improve semantic context, structure information clearly, build authority and measure visibility across different AI Search environments.

Does Schema Markup guarantee visibility in AI answers?

No. It can help search engines understand structured information, but there is no special Schema that guarantees visibility in AI-generated answers.
